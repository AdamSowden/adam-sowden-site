# Compliance Rules — Virtus Financial Group
## SEC Marketing Rule (Rule 206(4)-1) — Content Requirements

> This document governs all marketing content produced for Virtus Financial
> Group (Virtus Wealth Management LLC), a Registered Investment Advisor (RIA).
> All content must comply with the SEC Marketing Rule before publication.
> Claude must apply these rules to every piece of content produced.

---

## Governing Regulation

**SEC Marketing Rule: Rule 206(4)-1**
Effective: May 4, 2021 (compliance required from November 4, 2022)
Applies to: All RIA marketing and advertising content
Source: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2020/ia-5653.pdf

---

## The Seven General Prohibitions

The Marketing Rule prohibits any advertisement that:

1. Includes any untrue statement of a material fact
2. Includes a materially misleading implication through omission
3. Includes information that is misleading in the context it is presented
4. Discusses potential benefits without fair and balanced discussion of risks
5. References specific investment advice without reflecting full performance
6. Includes or excludes performance results in a misleading way
7. Is otherwise materially misleading

## Content Rules — What Claude Must Apply

### Factual Claims

**Allowed:**
- Historical market data with a clear time period stated
  Example: "$200,000 invested in the S&P 500 twenty years ago would be
  worth approximately $1.34 million today"
- General statistical ranges with appropriate hedging
  Example: "clients in similar situations have typically seen"
- Publicly available economic and tax information

**Not allowed:**
- Specific client performance results without full disclosure context
- Claims that imply guaranteed outcomes from investment strategies
- Statistics without a verifiable source basis
- Comparative claims against named competitors

---

### Hypothetical and Illustrative Scenarios

**Rule:** Any hypothetical performance scenario must include a clear
disclosure that it is illustrative only and not based on actual results.

**Required disclosure for any hypothetical scenario in blog content:**

> "The scenarios and figures presented are hypothetical and for
> illustrative purposes only. They do not represent actual client results
> and should not be relied upon as a prediction or guarantee of future
> performance."

**When this disclosure is required:**
- Any "Portfolio A vs Portfolio B" comparison
- Any projected dollar figures over time (e.g., "$1M growing to $X")
- Any income gap calculations using assumed figures
- Any tax savings projections
- Any scenario using named or unnamed client situations

**Placement:** This disclosure must appear immediately after the
hypothetical scenario in the body copy — not only in the footer.

**Current blog content using hypothetical scenarios:**
- Replace It: Income gap examples (Mark Taylor scenario)
- Grow It: Portfolio A vs Portfolio B ($1.5M comparison)
- Protect It: RMD stacking examples (Linda & Robert scenario)

---

### Client Stories and Testimonials

**Rule:** The Marketing Rule has specific requirements for testimonials
and endorsements including required disclosures about compensation and
whether the experience is representative.

**Current approach — COMPLIANT:**
All client scenarios in Virtus content use fictionalised composite
personas (Mark, Jim, Mike, Linda, Robert, Alex). These are illustrative
character sketches, not testimonials or endorsements from real clients.
This approach avoids the testimonial disclosure requirements entirely.

**If real client quotes or stories are ever used:**
The following disclosure would be required:
> "[Client name] is a client of Virtus Financial Group. This testimonial
> may not be representative of the experience of other clients and is not
> a guarantee of future performance or success."

**Rule:** Do not use real client names, identifiable details, or actual
client outcomes in content without explicit compliance review and the
required testimonial disclosure.

---

### Performance Claims and Returns

**Never allowed in any content:**
- Specific return percentages promised or implied
  Example: "We will achieve 8% annual returns for your portfolio"
- Claims that a specific strategy will produce a specific outcome
  Example: "This approach will save you $400,000 in taxes"
- Language implying certainty about future market performance
  Example: "Markets will recover" or "Stocks always go up long term"

**Allowed with appropriate hedging:**
- Historical market performance with time period stated
- General ranges with caveat language
  Example: "clients in this situation have typically seen tax savings
  ranging from tens of thousands to six figures depending on their
  specific circumstances"
- Reference to general industry research or data

---

### Guaranteed Income Language

**Special rule for insurance-based income:**
The word "guaranteed" may only be used in the context of income streams
structured through insurance products (annuities, life insurance), and
only when referring to the claims-paying ability of the issuing insurer.

**Allowed:**
"A guaranteed income stream structured through an insurance product"
"Guaranteed death benefit subject to the claims-paying ability of
the issuing insurance company"

**Not allowed:**
"We guarantee your retirement income"
"Guaranteed returns on your investment portfolio"
"Your portfolio is guaranteed to grow"

---

### Tax Claims

**Rule:** Tax outcomes vary by individual circumstance. Never present
tax strategies as guaranteed to produce specific savings.

**Allowed:**
"Proper tax planning can significantly reduce your tax burden over
a 20 to 30 year retirement horizon"
"Strategies like Roth conversions, when implemented correctly,
can reduce required minimum distribution exposure"

**Not allowed:**
"This strategy will save you $200,000 in taxes"
"Roth conversions will eliminate your tax liability"
"You will pay zero taxes in retirement"

---

### Balance Requirement

**Rule:** Content that discusses benefits of a strategy must provide
fair and balanced treatment of the risks and limitations.

**What this means in practice:**
- Blog posts that advocate for the Possibility Plan over the Survival
  Plan must acknowledge that outcomes depend on individual circumstances
- Content that critiques the 60/40 or 4% rule must be clearly framed
  as analytical perspective, not as guaranteed superior performance
- The Virtus approach must be presented as a framework, not a promise

**The standard disclaimer handles most of this — but body copy must
not make claims that directly contradict the disclaimer.**

---

### Prohibited Language: "Most People"

**Rule:** The phrase "most people" is not permitted in any content. Use
"many people" or "a lot of people" instead. This applies to blog posts,
email copy, and the chat widget.

The word "most" implies a statistical claim that cannot be substantiated
and may constitute a misleading generalisation under SEC guidance.

**Not allowed:**
"Most people don't have a retirement plan"
"Most financial advisors avoid tax planning"
"Most retirees are afraid to spend"

**Allowed:**
"Many people don't have a retirement plan"
"A lot of financial advisors avoid tax planning"
"Many retirees are afraid to spend"

## Required Disclosures

### Standard Footer Disclaimer
Must appear on every blog post, email, and marketing piece:

> "The content provided reflects personal opinions and general commentary
> and is intended for informational and educational purposes only. It
> should not be interpreted as individualized investment, tax, legal, or
> financial planning advice. Consult with a qualified financial
> professional before making any financial decisions."

### Hypothetical Performance Disclosure
Must appear immediately after any hypothetical scenario in body copy:

> "The scenarios and figures presented are hypothetical and for
> illustrative purposes only. They do not represent actual client results
> and should not be relied upon as a prediction or guarantee of future
> performance."

### RIA Registration Disclosure
Must appear on all marketing materials (typically in site footer):

> "Virtus Wealth Management LLC is a Registered Investment Advisor
> registered in the State of Missouri. Registration does not imply a
> certain level of skill or training."

## Record Keeping Requirement

**Rule:** All marketing content must be maintained for five years from
the date of publication.

**How the GitHub repo satisfies this:**
Every blog post committed to the virtus-site GitHub repository is
automatically timestamped and version-controlled. This creates a
permanent, auditable record of all published content and any subsequent
changes — satisfying the SEC's five-year record keeping requirement.

This is one of the reasons migrating from GHL to the GitHub-based
publishing pipeline is compliance-relevant, not just technical.

## Pre-Publication Compliance Checklist

Before any content is published, confirm:

- [ ] No specific return guarantees or implied promises
- [ ] No specific tax savings stated as a certainty
- [ ] All hypothetical scenarios include the hypothetical disclosure
- [ ] No real client names or identifiable details used
- [ ] No comparative claims against named competitors
- [ ] Guaranteed income language limited to insurance product context
- [ ] Footer disclaimer present
- [ ] Content is balanced — benefits discussed alongside limitations
- [ ] No statements that contradict or undermine the footer disclaimer
