# Founder's Edge HQ: Anti-Methodology

The approaches that Founder's Edge explicitly rejects. Each entry includes: the name, why it fails, the cost to the client, and the exact redirect line Ryll uses when a prospect is stuck in it.

---

## Rejected Approach 1: The Accountability Coach

**Name:** The Accountability Coach (also: "support and accountability without strategy")

**What it is:** A business coach who provides weekly or fortnightly check-ins, goal-setting conversations, and accountability to tasks the owner has already decided to do. The structure is: set goals, check in, repeat.

**Why it fails:** Accountability to the wrong actions produces the wrong results faster. Without a strategic leverage plan built from the business's actual financial data, the owner is simply staying busy in a direction no one has confirmed is the highest-return path. Effort substitutes for strategy.

**Cost to client:** Standard trajectory of 5 to 10 percent growth per year regardless of effort. The owner works harder, the coach nods encouragingly, and the business stays proportional to the owner's hours rather than compounding.

**Redirect line:** "I don't understand how coaches can coach without doing the strategic plan first. Support and accountability without strategy is staying busy in the wrong direction. You can't tell someone to work harder if you haven't first mapped the fastest, easiest, most profitable path to where they want to go."

## Rejected Approach 2: Common Wisdom

**Name:** Common Wisdom (also: "peer echo chamber," "advice from other people in the same boat")

**What it is:** Taking strategic direction from industry peers, local business networks, or social media voices who are operating at the same level and facing the same constraints. The shared assumption: "It's tough out there, you can't grow in this economy."

**Why it fails:** Peers confirm each other's limitations. The advice reflects what works at the median. The economy is a shared constraint. Businesses that apply a strategic growth plan inside that constraint outperform those that wait. The most common peer advice, lower your prices, just get through it, hold off on investing, is optimised for survival. Growth requires a different plan.

**Cost to client:** Two years of waiting for conditions to improve while competitors with a leverage strategy grow past them. The window does not reopen.

**Redirect line:** "The laws of business are immutable. They don't change with the economy. The question is whether you've built a business that applies those laws or one that just reacts to conditions."

## Rejected Approach 3: Learning From Your Own Mistakes

**Name:** The Solo Experimenter (also: "learning from your own mistakes instead of a proven recipe")

**What it is:** Building business strategy through trial and error, improvisation, and personal experience accumulated over time. The belief that experience is the only teacher worth trusting.

**Why it fails:** It is expensive and slow. Every mistake the owner makes to learn a lesson was already made by someone else. Paying full tuition for each lesson means the business builds slowly, the owner carries the mental load of every experiment, and the path to the right answer runs through years of wrong ones first.

**Cost to client:** A five-year road to results that a proven recipe delivers in twelve months. Plus the compounding cost of every year the business stayed smaller than it needed to be.

**Redirect line:** "There's a concept called R&D, rip off and duplicate. You don't have to make every mistake yourself. The fastest way to where you want to be is a proven recipe, not improvisation. That's what the Revenue Resource Map gives you."

## Rejected Approach 4: The Busy-Work Trap

**Name:** The Busy-Work Trap (also: "rise and grind," "work harder, do more")

**What it is:** The belief that putting in more hours, taking more calls, and staying busier will eventually produce the breakthrough. The default response to plateaued results is to push harder on the same actions.

**Why it fails:** A leaky bucket holds the same water regardless of how fast you pour. Effort is abundant. The limiting factor is strategy. More inputs into a system designed without a leverage plan produce the same output per unit of effort, every time.

**Cost to client:** Burnout, owner-dependent business, family time traded for marginal gains. The business hits a ceiling that effort alone cannot break through.

**Redirect line:** "How much water can you put in a leaky bucket? Why not change the bucket? Why not switch from a spike to an F-35? The problem isn't your effort, it's the strategic vehicle you're using."
