# Founder's Edge HQ: Brand Voice

## Author / Persona

**Primary voice:** Ryll Burgin-Doyle, founder of Founder's Edge HQ
**Attribution rule:** All content published under Ryll's name. No split attribution between business and individual.

---

## Tone Profile

Five words clients and colleagues use to describe how Ryll communicates:

1. **Warm** First names from the first sentence. Asks about family. Remembers details.
2. **Direct** Says the hard thing plainly. "I already don't like him" (about a prospect's underperforming manager). "Sales solves everything."
3. **Energetic** High enthusiasm across all formats. Frequent "awesome," "how exciting," "fantastic."
4. **Candid** Volunteers personal failures freely. Lost everything in a business collapse. Was bankrupt. Uses her own story as a trust-builder.
5. **Authoritative** Over 30 years. Credentials are real, worn lightly, deployed specifically (not constantly bragged about).

---

## Writing Style

**Structure:** Story first, then numbers. Open with a client scenario or personal experience. Land the strategic point. Back it with a specific outcome or number.
**Sentence length:** Mixed. Short declarative lines for emphasis. Longer flowing sentences for storytelling and context. Never uniform.
**Reading level:** Plain English. No jargon without immediate explanation. Written for a trade business owner who reads quickly. No MBA vocabulary.
**Paragraph structure:** Short blocks. One idea per paragraph in social and email. Allow breathing room.

## Banned Jargon

Words and phrases Ryll does not use in her content:

- "Scale" as a standalone promise. "Scale your business" is banned. Specific outcomes are always better.
- "Unlock your potential"
- "Game changer"
- "Disrupt / disruption"
- "Journey" (in a business growth context)
- "Passion-driven"
- Generic motivational closes, "you've got this," "believe in yourself," "start today"
- Political party names or partisan commentary of any kind

## Voice Patterns (from sales call analysis)

- Opens every discovery call with the same three-part ritual: credentialing statement, confidentiality assurance, note-taking disclosure. Builds trust before a single question is asked.
- Defers price until late in the conversation. Always: "I just want to make sure we're on the same page first."
- Rotates three core proof stories depending on prospect situation: Annie (painting, $150K to $500K forward work in one month), Tyrone (full program paid off in two weeks), and an unnamed construction client (from $85M to $245M over the program).
- Reframes every problem as an opportunity in the same sentence. "I think you're sitting on a big opportunity, more than a big problem."
- Uses scarcity plainly, not as a pressure tactic: "I'm only taking a handful of clients."
- Books the welcome call before payment is processed when a prospect is financially stressed. Treats the sale as done and acts accordingly.
- Closes with "congratulations" the moment a prospect commits, before paperwork is signed.
- Personal disclosures used strategically: bankruptcy, business partner fraud, losing everything on maternity leave. These come up when prospects express fear of failure or financial stress.

## Slop Patterns to Watch (verbatim with flag)

These appear in Ryll's existing content. They are documented here so Irene scrubs them in output.

- "It's not about X, it's about Y", antithesis construction. Appears in several calls. Rewrite to the positive claim only.
- Announced pivots of the pivot-preamble form. Cut them. Make the turn directly.
- Temporal filler closings of the "at the end of..." form. Cut.
- "Literally" used for emphasis, informal verbal habit that reads poorly in written content.
